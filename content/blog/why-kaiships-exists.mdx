I did not want to start another AI-guru brand. The space is drowning in them - screenshot threads, "10 prompts that will 10x your life," courses that are a Notion doc with a checkout page. I almost walked away from the whole niche. Then I actually measured it, and the numbers pointed somewhere the hype-sellers were not standing.

## What the research corpus actually said

Before writing a word of copy, I built a research corpus - posts, comments, and threads from where Claude Code power users actually hang out - and looked at what earned attention versus what got ignored.

Two things jumped out.

First: in the sample I measured, skepticism out-reached tutorials by around 146 times. Posts poking holes in an agent demo, asking "does this actually work," calling out slop - those traveled. Clean tutorials mostly sat there. The audience is not short on instructions. They are short on trust.

Second: the single best-performing technical hook in my sample was a cost teardown - taking a Claude Code job from about 42 dollars down to about 6 on the same machine, shown with receipts. Not a promise, not a flex. A verifiable before-and-after. People did not want to be told it was cheaper. They wanted to watch it get cheaper.

Put those together and the position writes itself. This crowd rewards proof and punishes hype. So the brand has to be the opposite of the guru playbook.

## Installable files, not prompt lists

The other thing the research made obvious: people in this niche do not want a list of prompts to copy by hand. They want files they can install and read. A prompt list is something you have to trust and retype. A CLAUDE.md, a skill folder, an agent definition - that is something you drop in, inspect line by line, and run. You can verify it before it touches anything.

That is the whole product philosophy. The free PACK is not a lead-magnet PDF. It is real files - a curated CLAUDE.md, five skills, two agents - that you install and audit yourself. Nothing runs blind. If you do not trust a piece, you read it first, or you delete it.

## The anti-slop counter-position

So here is where kaiships stands, on purpose:

- **Verify over viral.** Every claim on this site should come with a way to check it. Where something can fail, I say so. Cost and time numbers are framed as examples from my machine, not guarantees.
- **Files over prompts.** You get installable, inspectable things, not screenshots to retype.
- **Honest over impressive.** No invented member counts. No fake testimonials. No revenue flexes. If I have not shipped it and checked it, it does not go up.

That is a narrower market than "everyone who wants to make money with AI." Good. The research says the people who actually build with Claude Code are allergic to the wide version. They have seen it. They scrolled past it 146 times for every tutorial they saved.

## What this means for the site

Everything here is built to be checked. The resources are real setups you install. The ship log is what I actually did, including what broke. The community exists so this compounds week over week - but I will get to the honest math of that in another post, because pretending a solo community is a money-printer would be its own kind of slop.

kaiships exists because the gap in this niche is not more content. It is someone who verifies. That is the entire bet.
