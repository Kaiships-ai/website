The standard creator playbook is a ladder: free thing, cheap thing, mid thing, then a high-ticket done-for-you offer at 2,997 dollars that is where the real money supposedly lives. I cut the ladder down to two rungs on purpose. Here is the math, honestly, including the part that could sink it.

## The two rungs

- **Rung one: the free PACK.** Real installable files - a curated CLAUDE.md, five skills, two agents. Free, forever, no upsell hidden inside it.
- **Rung two: the community.** 97 dollars a month, on Discord, with weekly skill and agent drops. Founding price is 47 dollars a month for the first roughly 50 members, grandfathered for as long as they stay.

That is it. No 500-dollar course, no 2,997-dollar done-for-you.

## Why I cut the high-ticket rung

Because I have not validated it, and adding an unvalidated rung is just slop with a bigger price tag. A done-for-you tier is a promise to hand-build agent systems for individual clients. I could sell that. But I would be selling a delivery capacity I have not proven, at a price that invites expectations I have not tested. The honest move is to cut it until the community tells me it should exist - not to bolt it on because the playbook says the money is there.

Every rung is also a thing I have to maintain, support, and keep honest. Two rungs I can run alone and verify. Four rungs is a job I would do badly.

## The math I actually have to hit

A subscription community is not a launch, it is a treadmill. The only numbers that matter are what comes in each month and how fast it leaks out. So the model is simple to write and hard to run:

Monthly recurring revenue is members times price. The founding cohort is capped near 50 at 47 dollars; after that the price is 97. I am not going to post a member count here, because I will not invent one and I will not flex a real one - the point is the shape of the math, not a scoreboard.

The number that decides everything is churn. If people leave faster than the weekly drops give them a reason to stay, the treadmill wins. My working line is this: churn above roughly 20 percent a month kills it. At that rate you are refilling a bucket that empties faster than you can pour, and every month is spent replacing people instead of compounding. Below it, the drops have to be good enough that staying is the obvious call.

That is the entire business risk in one variable. Not traffic, not launches - retention.

## Why weekly drops are the whole strategy

The weekly skill and agent drops are not a content calendar. They are the anti-churn mechanism. A community you paid for once and forgot is a cancellation waiting for the next credit-card review. A community that shipped you something useful this week - a new skill you installed, an agent that saved you an afternoon - is one you keep. The drops exist to make the math above survivable. If a week's drop is filler, that is not a missed post; it is churn I earned.

## The honest version

Here is what I am not claiming. I am not claiming a member count. I am not claiming revenue. I am not claiming this will work - a solo community with a churn problem is a real way to fail, and I would rather say that out loud than sell you certainty I do not have.

What I am claiming is the structure is honest: two rungs I can actually deliver, a founding price that rewards the people who show up early, and a single number - retention - that I have to earn every week or the whole thing unwinds. No high-ticket rung papering over an unproven middle. When the community says the next rung should exist, I will build it and verify it. Until then, two rungs, no slop.
